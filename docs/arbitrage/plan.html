The arbitrage plan

How we find sportsbook arbitrage across the NFL, NBA, NHL and MLB, what a month of saved prices shows, and what has to be true before a pair is worth betting. The short version: pairs appear most days, but they are small, and from 2026 federal tax can turn a 1% pair into a loss.

The short version

They exist

Over the replayed month, pairs that paid the same either way showed up in every league. The typical one returned about 1% of the amount staked.

Few last

Most were gone by the next refresh. The ones that lasted mostly involved offshore books, or MLB batter props whose settlement rules differ between books.

Tax sets the bar

Only 90% of gambling losses are deductible from tax year 2026. At the 24% bracket, a pair needs roughly 1.6% just to break even after federal tax if you itemize. Without itemizing, almost none do.

So the plan is narrow on purpose: bet only locked pairs, at licensed books, above your after-tax break-even, after confirming both prices yourself, and record both bets. The Arbitrage finder applies these rules and its calculator shows the after-tax result for each pair.

What counts as an arbitrage here

An arbitrage is two bets at two sportsbooks where every final result pays back at least what you staked. The finder checks three shapes, and grades each one on every result that matters (the final margin, or the player’s or game’s count) rather than trusting a shortcut:

  • Two-way: both sides of one line, such as Over 21.5 at one book and Under 21.5 at another, or each team’s moneyline.
  • Middle: two lines that overlap, such as Over 24.5 and Under 26.5. Either way one wins, and a 25 or 26 wins both.
  • Moneyline against a spread: one team to win and the other team +1.5. If the favorite wins by exactly one, both win.

Write p for a price’s break-even probability (100/(odds+100) for plus odds, odds/(odds−100) for minus odds, using the absolute value). If the two add to less than 1, stake each side in proportion to its p: for a total stake S, bet S × p₁ / (p₁ + p₂) and S × p₂ / (p₁ + p₂). A single winning side then returns S / (p₁ + p₂). A result exactly on a whole-number line refunds both bets.

A pair is Locked only when all of these hold:

  • the two quotes were taken within five minutes of each other, before the game started;
  • they are at two different sportsbooks;
  • both books’ settlement rules for that market are on file and match (overtime, ties, a player who doesn’t play). Rules can be checked against the book’s published rules or assumed standard; assumed ones are labeled.

Anything else under 100% is listed under Check before betting with the reason. Pairs between 100% and 101% are near misses. Nothing on these pages feeds Today’s Picks or Market Watch.

Taxes decide the threshold

Every winning bet’s profit is gambling income. Every losing bet is a gambling loss, and from tax year 2026 only 90% of losses are deductible, only if you itemize, and never more than your winnings. An arbitrage always has a losing side, so tax applies to more than your profit. For a pair with profit P whose losing bet was L, at federal rate t:

  • itemizing: after-tax profit ≈ (1 − t) × P − t × 0.10 × L;
  • standard deduction: after-tax profit ≈ (1 − t) × P − t × L.

With the losing bet about half the stake, the return a pair needs before tax just to break even is about:

How to work it

  1. Set up. Open and fund accounts at the licensed sportsbooks available where you are. Each pair needs money already sitting at both books, so plan on a balance at every book you use. Ask a tax professional whether you will itemize and at what rate, then set those in the finder’s calculator. The finder can hide pairs that involve offshore books; we recommend you do.
  2. Pilot by hand, two to four weeks. Check the finder after each refresh. Bet only Locked pairs whose after-tax result is positive, at $100 to $250 per pair. Before betting, open both books and confirm the line and both prices. Place the bet at the book whose price stands out from the others first, because it is the one most likely to move, then the other side at the price shown or better. Track both bets in Bet Tracker with the finder’s Track buttons.
  3. Keep score. Log every attempt, including the ones where the second price had moved, a book cut your stake or a bet was refused. The pilot’s question is simple: after failed attempts and tax, did it make money?
  4. Scale only on evidence. Raise stakes toward each book’s limits only if the pilot was positive after tax. Books often limit accounts that only bet arbitrage; when one does, stop routing pairs to it.

When something goes wrong

  • The second price moved. You now hold one side alone. Either take the other side at the best price still available, accepting a small loss, or decide up front that you will hold. Decide before you start, not in the moment.
  • A bet is voided. If one book voids its side (a player scratch, an obvious-error price, a rule you missed) and the other does not, the remaining bet is unhedged. Matching settlement rules are why Locked requires them.
  • Caps. Set a maximum per pair and a maximum total of unhedged bets per day, and stop for the day when it is reached.

What the profiles mean

  • Includes overtime; a tied moneyline is refunded (NFL): Moneylines, spreads and totals are graded on the final score including overtime. A two-way moneyline on a game that ends tied is a push.
  • Overtime counts; void if the player doesn't play (NFL): Player props count the whole game including overtime. A player who does not play is void, not a loss.
  • Includes overtime (NBA): Moneylines, spreads and totals are graded on the final score including overtime.
  • Overtime counts; void if the player doesn't play (NBA): Player props count the whole game including overtime. A player who does not play is void, not a loss.
  • Includes overtime and shootout (NHL): Game bets (moneyline, puck line, total) are graded on the final score including overtime and the shootout; a shootout win adds one goal to the winning team's score.
  • Overtime counts, shootout does not; void if the player doesn't play (NHL): Player props count statistics from regulation and overtime, not the shootout. The player must take part in the game; a player who does not play is void, not a loss.
  • Action; official after 5 innings (MLB): Moneylines stand whoever pitches and are official after five innings (4½ if the home team leads). Run lines and totals need nine innings (8½ if the home team leads).

Kinds of sportsbook

  • Licensed: Licensed by US state regulators where it operates.
  • Offshore: Not licensed by any US state. Legal, payout and account risks are yours.
  • Sweepstakes: A sweepstakes or social sportsbook, not a state-licensed sportsbook.

What this does not model

Bet limits, account restrictions, state availability, obvious-error cancellations, rounding to whole dollars, delays between a book’s price and our feed, and partial fills are not modeled.
