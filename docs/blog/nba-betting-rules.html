NBA Betting Rules: Overtime, Player Props and the Player Who Sits

In the NBA, the rule that decides the most bets isn’t about the score. It’s about who plays. Stars are ruled out an hour before tipoff, starters sit on the second night of a back-to-back, and a player can tweak an ankle four minutes into the game.

Each of those has a rule in your sportsbook’s house rules, and so does overtime. Here is how the standard rules at major U.S. sportsbooks work, and where books differ.

1. Overtime counts for full-game bets

Moneylines, spreads and totals are graded on the final score including every overtime period. NBA games cannot end in a tie, so a moneyline is always decided.

Overtime matters most for totals. Say the Knicks and Celtics are tied 108–108 at the end of regulation, and you have under 221.5. Regulation produced 216 points, so you were winning. But overtime counts, and five more minutes of basketball has to produce five or fewer combined points for your under to survive.

5 or fewer221 or lessWins
6 or more222 or moreLoses

Spreads work the same way. A 7.5-point favorite that wins 120–112 in overtime covers, even though the game was tied after 48 minutes.

2. Half and quarter bets: read the label

First-half and first-quarter bets end long before overtime. Later periods are where books can differ:

  • Second half: usually includes overtime.
  • Fourth quarter: often graded on the fourth quarter alone, without overtime.

If the market name doesn’t say, the house rules do. Check before you assume two books are selling the same bet.

3. Player props: once he takes the court, it stands

Standard player props count the player’s full-game statistics, including overtime. A player who scores 6 points in overtime can turn an under into a loss.

Participation decides even more props than overtime does:

  • He never enters the game (ruled out, rested, or a coach’s decision): the bet is void and your stake is returned, whether you bet the over or the under.
  • He plays any time at all: the bet stands. FanDuel’s rule, for example, says bets stand once a player takes the court, regardless of how long he plays.

That means a player who plays two minutes and leaves with a sprained ankle is graded on two minutes. If you bet his over 24.5 points, you lose. If you bet the under, you win. An ejection works the same way.

Late scratches and parlays

NBA injury news often lands close to tipoff. If you bet a player’s prop and he is ruled out, a straight bet is simply void. In a parlay, the void leg is usually dropped and the rest of the parlay pays at shorter odds. Books do not all handle same-game parlays the same way, so check yours.

Combined props, such as points + rebounds + assists, follow the same rules: they count overtime, and they stand once the player takes the court.

4. Postponed games

If a game is postponed and not played within the book’s deadline, bets are void. Each book sets its own deadline.

Why this matters when you compare prices

Comparing prices only works when the bets are the same. A fourth-quarter line that excludes overtime is a different bet from one that includes it, and the price difference between them is not value. The same goes for removing a book’s margin to estimate a fair probability: you can only devig two sides that are graded the same way.

Before you shop a line, check that each book is selling the same bet. Then the price comparison means something.

A quick checklist before you bet

  • Does it include overtime? Full-game bets and player props: yes. Second half: usually. Fourth quarter: check.
  • What if he doesn’t play? Void.
  • What if he plays two minutes and leaves? The bet stands.
  • How are void parlay legs handled? Usually dropped, but check, especially for same-game parlays.
  • Which state are you in? House rules can differ by jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Does overtime count in NBA over/under bets?

Yes. Full-game totals, spreads and moneylines are graded on the final score including every overtime period.

Do NBA player props include overtime?

Yes. Standard player props count the player’s full-game statistics, including overtime.

What happens to an NBA player prop if the player does not play?

If the player never enters the game, the bet is void and the stake is returned. Once he takes the court, the bet stands regardless of how long he plays, even if he is injured or ejected.

Do NBA second-half and fourth-quarter bets include overtime?

Second-half bets usually include overtime. Fourth-quarter bets are often graded on the fourth quarter alone. Check the market name and your book’s house rules.

This guide describes common rules at major U.S. sportsbooks as of October 2026. Rules change and differ by book and state. Your sportsbook’s published house rules decide how your bet is graded.
