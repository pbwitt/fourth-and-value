Home Field Advantage in Player Props: Small for Yards, Bigger for Touchdowns

Analysis, not a guarantee. Picks and projections are uncertain; odds and player information can change. No outcome or profit is guaranteed. Any wager is your decision and responsibility. Read our limitations and responsible-play guidance.

Everyone knows home field matters. For player props, the useful question is how much, stat by stat. A quarterback’s passing yards, a receiver’s catches and a running back’s chance of scoring don’t all move by the same amount when the game is at home.

We measured it on NFL games from 2012 through 2025. The short answer: home field barely moves yardage, and it moves touchdowns quite a bit more.

The team-level edge

Historically, NFL home teams have outscored visitors by roughly 2 to 3 points per game. Several analyses found the edge noticeably smaller from 2019 on, and almost nothing in 2020, when most games were played in empty stadiums. Point spreads already build this in.

What home field does to player stats

For each stat, we compared players’ actual output with what was expected, separately at home and away, leaving out neutral-site games. Then we tested the fitted factors on later seasons the fit never saw. This is what we found:

The likely range is a 95% interval. Where it crosses zero, as for receptions, pass attempts, completions and interceptions, we can’t say the effect is real at all. The full method and data are in our research report, Player Matchups and Home Field.

What that looks like in a projection

Yardage barely moves. A quarterback projected for 280 passing yards at a neutral site projects to about 284 at home and 276 on the road. That 8-yard swing is small next to how much a quarterback’s output varies from game to game.

Touchdowns move more. Take a running back with a 40% chance to score at a neutral site, a fair price of +150:

VenueChance to scoreFair price
Home42.2%+137
Neutral40.0%+150
Away37.9%+164

A swing from +137 to +164 is real money on a touchdown prop, while the same venue effect is a rounding error on most yardage lines. We can’t say for certain why scoring moves more than yardage. One plausible reason is that touchdowns are the payoff of whole drives, so a small edge on each play compounds by the time a team reaches the end zone.

“He’s a different player at home”

Bettors often point to a player’s personal home/away split. We tested that too. Once the league-wide venue effects above were in the forecast, a player’s own split added nothing measurable, and the same was true of his history against a particular opponent.

The reason is sample size. A player gets eight or nine home games a season. A split built on a few dozen games is mostly noise, and in our tests it didn’t carry forward to future games.

Other sports

  • MLB: per plate appearance, home batters get about 2% more hits, 3% more home runs and 7% more walks (including hit-by-pitches), and strike out about 4% less, from the same study.
  • NHL: we tested personal venue splits for hockey as well, and they are not inputs to our model.
  • NBA: we have not yet measured venue effects for NBA player props. Until we do, the same caution applies: a player’s personal home/away split from one season is mostly noise.

What it means for your bets

  • Don’t move a yardage projection much for venue: a percent or two, not five or ten.
  • Do account for it on touchdown props, where it is worth a couple of percentage points of probability.
  • Ignore personal splits and “owns this team” narratives. They didn’t predict anything in our tests.
  • Remember the books know this too. The edge is not knowing that home field exists; it is sizing it correctly when a line doesn’t.

Frequently asked questions

How much does home field advantage affect NFL player props?

Less than most bettors assume for yardage. Measured on 2012–2025 games, home teams gain about 1.4% in passing and receiving yards and about 2.4% in rushing yards. Touchdowns move more, about 5%.

Do player home and away splits predict props?

In our testing, no. Once league-wide venue effects were in the forecast, a player’s own home/away split added nothing measurable. Eight or nine home games a season is too small a sample to separate a real split from noise.

How many points is NFL home field advantage worth?

Historically, home teams outscored visitors by roughly 2 to 3 points per game. Several analyses found the edge smaller since 2019.
