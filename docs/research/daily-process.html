How our daily picks are produced

Analysis, not a guarantee. Picks and projections are uncertain; odds and player information can change. No outcome or profit is guaranteed. Any wager is your decision and responsibility. Read our limitations and responsible-play guidance.

We combine independent sports forecasts, available sportsbook prices and sourced research to build a list of potential bets to review before deciding. The process can return no bets. All three sports remain experimental for betting decisions; a completed review does not establish a profitable edge.

Policy: morning-edition-1 · All times are America/New_York, including daylight saving. This page is generated from the workflow configuration and checked for drift in pull requests.

The simple flow: Refresh sports data and prices → run the models → research the slate independently → combine the candidate pools → check the price and sourced context → publish up to 10 reviewed ideas → you decide and track.

When the work runs

These are scheduled starts, not guaranteed publication times. Jobs can queue, feeds can fail and books can post markets later. A research job waits for the relevant sports refresh when triggered by that refresh; it does not assume that a scheduled job has finished. The quote timestamp in the picks table is the sportsbook’s timestamp, never the page refresh time.

The order inside a successful run

  1. Refresh sports data and prices. The sports job retrieves its configured statistics, schedule and authorized odds feeds, retains stable identities and quote times, and archives inputs. This is not a claim that every injury, lineup or weather observation is a numerical model input.
  2. Run the sport’s model pipeline. Current inputs produce independent quantity forecasts and outcome probabilities where supported. NHL uses versioned trained models; MLB refreshes its cached daily training bundle as needed; NFL refreshes player/history estimates and its scoring projections. Missing models stay missing. The data and model update finishes before the review job consumes its published board.
  3. Research the slate independently. A separate AI-assisted search sees the available games and market types, without our model predictions or rankings. It looks for opportunity, matchup and participation questions. Suggestions must resolve to a real current offer with the exact game, player, market, side, line and book. Search suggestions are unverified leads until the underlying dated reporting is retrieved. Each run makes at most one bounded discovery request, with games submitted and source coverage recorded separately. A large slate may not fit; unsubmitted games have not received this independent search. The later candidate-review stage uses multiple batches. A game being included in a search request does not mean every market or report was researched.
  4. Build the union of candidates. Keep offers passing the sport’s quantitative screen, plus independently researched offers. Neither route must agree with the other. Model quality gates, quote freshness and exact identities apply. The best available price is selected for an identical outcome and line. There is no four-pick quota and no one-bet-per-game rule.
  5. Collect evidence and investigate the case. Retrieve dated league/team reporting, injury information and relevant search leads. Check expected opportunity, starters, lineups, opponent effects, weather and settlement where evidence is available. Compare the projected quantity with the offered line, the model probability with the actual price, and the offered threshold with other books. Follow-up questions and unresolved source failures are retained. Professional betting recommendations are opinions, not verified support by themselves.
  6. Assess and rank. Each completed assessment says consider, wait for a specific material fact, or pass, with its case and countercase. Current consider assessments precede pending/wait/pass rows across all sports, so completed MLB or NHL opportunities remain visible above unreviewed NFL rows. The research pool retains within-sport rankings; the published card uses the common ranking described below. Related bets are labeled as shared exposure. No joint-bet probabilities are obtained by multiplying marginal chances.
  7. Build a manageable daily card. Publish up to 10 reviewed ideas, aiming for five to ten when the evidence supports them, with no minimum. At publication, only exact current consider assessments no older than three hours or explicit analyst selections qualify. Explicit analyst selections take priority. Consolidate alternate thresholds and equivalent bets; keep their original forecasts intact. Wait, pass, unreviewed and changed-price assessments stay in the separate collapsed research pool. Additional reviewed opportunities also remain there. Multiple bets in one game are permitted but carry shared-exposure labels. This card limit is a workflow choice, not evidence of profitability.
  8. Publish the review record. Preserve the original model, offer, source times, review, exclusions and budget usage. The table shows the actual book’s line and odds, quote time, model and market comparison, review status and tracker action. An independent research candidate without an eligible model says “Not established”; it receives no invented probability or EV.
  9. Keep the edition and track decisions. Save an immutable, dated card with original offers, forecasts and reviews. Later odds updates cannot erase it or silently approve a changed price. The research pool remains separate and continues to check freshness. A repeated morning run cannot replace a completed morning edition or spend again unless an operator explicitly requests replacement. A test edition does not complete or block the normal morning run; its archive and spending are retained. Incomplete research can recover during the morning window under the same daily budget. Manual afternoon tests are labeled test editions. Bets can be logged in Bet Tracker using the actual executed price; the site does not place wagers. Tracked bets settle automatically from final box scores: a check runs every 30 minutes and settles a bet once its game is final and started at least 4 hours ago. Bets it cannot match with certainty stay pending.

Contributor articles require manual publication

Reader suggestions and other articles submitted through the private Editorial Desk remain private until an editor reviews a complete saved draft, chooses Publish saved article, and confirms that version. Requesting research, saving text, switching to Opinion, or selecting homepage placement never authorizes publication. Analysis and Opinion use the same Generate draft, review, rewrite and manual Publish controls. Opinion research checks factual claims against current reporting without requiring betting lines or a model forecast. The hourly publisher only delivers explicitly approved versions and honors their earliest publication dates. Editing an approved version withdraws its approval. This is separate from the automatic daily analysis articles described above.

The formulas and selection rules

Let d be decimal odds, p the unconditional chance of winning and u the chance of a refunded push. Then loss probability is 1 − p − u.

  • Expected return per dollar: EV = p × (d − 1) − (1 − p − u) = p × d + u − 1.
  • Fair decimal price: d_fair = (1 − u) / p. A price’s break-even probability, conditional on no push, is 1 / d.
  • Two-way de-vig: for paired opposite prices, q_i = (1 / d_i) / [(1 / d_1) + (1 / d_2)]. Only matching identities, thresholds, settlement and timestamp windows can be compared. A median line is not the book’s expected outcome.

Freshness, coverage and cost

NFL quotes expire after 90 minutes. MLB quotes and model checks expire after 90 minutes. NHL Top Picks quotes and forecasts expire after 30 minutes, with model inputs checked within 36 hours. These checks apply when publishing and to the live research pool. Started games, future-dated quotes and failed model feeds cannot create new picks. Published editions retain their original timestamps and label started games as historical assessments. The research pool checks expiry every 30 seconds and reloads feeds every five minutes.

All Top Picks discovery and review share a $2.75 per Eastern calendar day allowance. It includes search and review requests, legacy same-day charges and conservative reservations for failures with unknown usage. The full $2.75 is available to morning research; there is no automatic later research run or reserved afternoon allowance. Unspent amounts do not roll over. Existing odds-feed costs and the separate daily-article writer are outside this research allowance. No new paid subscriptions are enabled. A request is skipped if its conservative maximum cannot fit; this can leave money unused and candidates awaiting review.

Research batches rotate across sports. Independent discovery ideas receive an early review turn; explicit replacement runs prioritize previously reviewed offers with changed facts or prices. This schedules research without assigning a higher win probability to those ideas. The main card holds at most 10 reviewed ideas. The separate collapsed research pool initially shows 20 additional offers, with a button to show all; all eligible candidates remain available for research. A research batch limit controls API payload size, not candidate eligibility. Budget and source limits can leave incomplete research. That coverage is recorded explicitly. No system can promise to examine every possible market exhaustively for $2.75, and no daily picks minimum is imposed.

One-day release test: The owner authorized a $20 shared research ceiling for September 27, 2026 only, including spending already recorded that day. It expires automatically at midnight Eastern; the usual $2.75 daily allowance resumes September 28. This does not change the maximum of ten reviewed ideas on the main card.

Ordering the daily card: After the completed-review gate, explicit analyst selections lead, followed by forecast reliability and an expected-log-growth score on a common scale across sports. The calculation uses the NHL ranker’s fixed 0.0025 fraction; it is an ordering convention, not staking advice. NFL uses the lower raw/calibrated estimate where available, MLB uses its win/push distribution and NHL retains its adverse-scenario score. Missing independent models receive no numerical score. Sport names and feed order do not receive priority, and no sport gets a guaranteed slot. This ordering has not been validated as a profitable selection strategy.

Each research run reviews at most eight batches of up to three candidates. Additional candidates remain in the research pool; reaching this limit is recorded separately from exhausting the daily budget.

What remains to prove

New independent discovery and review decisions are prospective shadow records. We have not shown that this larger candidate pool, new ordering or qualitative intervention improves returns. Grade original forecasts and reviewed selections separately, retain correlated exposure and execution prices, and assess forecast calibration, returns, drawdown and closing-price diagnostics where trustworthy data exist.
