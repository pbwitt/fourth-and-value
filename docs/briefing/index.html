    How this review list is selected

    First, the numbers. Each sport compares its model forecast with the price offered by a book. We retain all eligible candidates and the best observed book for each identical outcome and line. Different bets in the same game remain visible with a shared-exposure note. The rankings are within each sport, and there is no daily minimum. Market Watch is separate: a difference between books alone does not qualify a bet here.

    • NFL: forecasts with fitted historical-outcome calibration, using the lower of the raw and calibrated chance. A calibrated estimate of exactly 50% says nothing about this line and is withheld, as are unsupported calibration tails and possible partial-game workload distortions.
    • MLB: markets that passed the existing predictive checks, at least 3% model-estimated return, at least two paired books, and the best observed price at the same line.
    • NHL: an independent forecast, verified settlement mapping, at least 2% model-estimated return, and a price that still qualifies under the model’s adverse scenarios.

    Then, the market check. Our models have been more confident than the betting market, so every model candidate is blended with it: 25% our model, 75% the market’s no-vig chance at the exact same line, which at least two books (counting the listed one) must post. The blended chance gives the expected value at the listed price. 3% or more is a high-confidence pick. 1% to 3% is a moderate-confidence pick, with less cushion if the model is too optimistic. Below 1% is not a pick; each sport’s best positive one is shown as a lean. Blended returns above 30% get a lower research priority. Confidence here describes the size of the blended edge, not a chance of winning, and neither tier has been validated as profitable.

    Independent discovery. A separate review of the available slate receives no model rankings or probabilities. Its ideas join the candidate pool only when matched to a real current sportsbook offer. Missing or withheld models remain explicitly labeled; research never supplies a numerical edge.

    Then, our analysis. We assess the model assumptions, the quoted price and relevant dated reporting. Each completed assessment says consider, wait for a specific material check, or pass, with reasons. A favorable news story is not required; missing information matters when it could change the betting case. At publication, the main card admits current consider assessments or an explicit analyst selection, up to ten distinct ideas, with at most four per sport and two per sport and market. High-confidence picks are listed before moderate-confidence ones. Alternate lines and equivalent bets are consolidated. Wait, pass and unreviewed offers remain in the collapsed research pool, which also preserves additional reviewed ideas beyond the card limit. There is no minimum and no one-per-game rule. These experimental assessments do not change model probabilities or automatically approve bets.

    NFL’s model uses player history and calibration against historical results; current consensus is a separate comparison. Its calibration has not established a prospective betting edge. All three sports remain experimental for betting decisions. The model prediction is an expected quantity where available, not a guaranteed result or a market line.

    At publication, NFL quotes and MLB models/quotes must be within 90 minutes; NHL quotes/forecasts within 30 minutes, with model inputs checked within 36 hours. Started games and failed feeds cannot create new picks. Later market updates do not rewrite the published card or carry its assessment onto a new price. The separate research pool checks expiry and reloads every five minutes. Started games remain on the dated card as historical assessments. MLB hitter props may appear later when batting orders are published.
