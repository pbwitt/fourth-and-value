How a Kalshi price works

A Kalshi event contract pays $1 if the outcome happens and nothing if it doesn’t. The price is what you pay for that $1. Buy “Yes” at 60¢ and you risk 60¢ to win 40¢. Buy “No” on the same contract and you pay the other side, around 40¢, to win 60¢.

That is the same trade as a sportsbook bet, just quoted differently. A sportsbook says “−150”: risk $150 to win $100. Kalshi says “60¢”: risk 60¢ to win 40¢. Both are a 1.5-to-1 favorite’s price.

Converting cents to American odds

P ≤ 50¢: odds = +100 × (100 − P) / P
P > 50¢: odds = −100 × P / (100 − P)

So 40¢ is +150, 50¢ is even money (+100), 60¢ is −150 and 75¢ is −300. Going the other way, a sportsbook price converts to cents the same way it converts to an implied probability: −110 is 110 / 210 = 52.38¢, and +150 is 100 / 250 = 40¢.

What fees do to the price

The headline price is not what you pay. Kalshi’s published general taker fee is:

fee = 0.07 × contracts × P × (1 − P), rounded up to the next cent

The fee is largest near 50¢ and shrinks toward the extremes. Here is what it does to ten contracts at the 7% rate:

Comparing Kalshi with a sportsbook

To compare fairly, compare the all-in prices. A sportsbook’s −110 already includes its margin, and you need to win 52.38% of the time to break even. A Kalshi contract has to cost less than that after fees to be the better price.

At the 7% rate, a Kalshi contract beats −110 only at 50¢ or less. At 51¢, the fee brings the all-in cost to about 52.8¢, a worse price than −110. At 50¢, it is about 51.8¢, roughly −107.

Make sure you are comparing the same bet, too. Kalshi contracts settle under their own rules, which can differ from a sportsbook’s house rules. Some NFL contracts pay 50¢ on a tie, for example, where a sportsbook moneyline would push and refund your stake. Read the contract’s rules before you treat the two as interchangeable. Frequently asked questions

How do you convert a Kalshi price to American odds?

A contract pays $1, so a price of P cents risks P to win 100 − P. At 50¢ or less, American odds are +100 × (100 − P) / P. Above 50¢, they are −100 × P / (100 − P). A 60¢ contract is −150 before fees.

How are Kalshi fees calculated?

Kalshi’s published general taker fee is 0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up to the next cent. Some markets use a different rate. Ten contracts at 60¢ cost $6.00 plus a $0.17 fee.

Is a Kalshi price the probability of the event?

No. The all-in price is the break-even win rate you need to profit, not a forecast. Fees raise it, and contract settlement rules can differ from a sportsbook’s.

What Kalshi price beats −110 at a sportsbook?

With the 7% general taker fee, 50¢ or less. At 51¢ the fee pushes the all-in cost to about 52.8¢, a worse price than −110, which breaks even at 52.38%.
