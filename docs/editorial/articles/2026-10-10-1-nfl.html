An unresolved quarterback, not an automatic adjustment

Caleb Williams' potential return is the actionable uncertainty for Sunday's Chicago Bears–Green Bay Packers matchup. CBS's October 9 injury report lists the Bears quarterback as questionable with a hamstring injury after missing two weeks. It says he was limited Friday following two days without practice, and that Tyson Bagent handled the previous game. That establishes uncertainty about the position, not confirmation that Williams will start or play without restrictions.

The matchup also arrives after the announcement of Mike Ditka's death. CBS and NFL.com both report the family's announcement; those accounts represent one underlying announcement, not independent confirmation of a football effect. The loss is significant to Chicago's history, but our evidence offers no basis for translating emotion or a tribute narrative into additional points. The analytical question remains how much uncertainty a fixed total and its different prices ask readers to accept.

Same total, different transaction

Our October 10 snapshot has all ten observed books at 45.5 combined points for the October 11 game. The matched-book comparison with October 9 at 20:55 UTC shows no change in that number. This is a stored comparison, not an opening line, and it does not establish what happened between observations. Nor can we say the injury report caused any pricing response.

The selected quotes nevertheless differ materially. At approximately 11:10 UTC, FanDuel and MyBookie.ag offered both sides at -110. DraftKings listed the over at -118 and the under at -102; BetOnline.ag showed -113 and -107, respectively. Thus, agreement on the threshold does not mean agreement on the cost of taking either side.

A -110 wager requires a 52.38% winning rate to break even, compared with 54.13% at -118 and 50.50% at -102. Those are contractual break-even rates, not estimates of the game's outcome. For someone already considering the over, paying -118 rather than an available -110 raises the hurdle without buying a lower total. The reverse price comparison favors the -102 under, but a cheaper transaction alone does not establish a sound bet. Williams' unresolved status remains relevant whichever price is selected.

What the dated estimate can—and cannot—tell us

Fourth & Value's saved experimental scoring estimate, using information through October 5, put combined points at 46.14: approximately 21.98 for Green Bay and 24.16 for Chicago. It has not been validated against betting prices. This is dated background, not a fresh prediction, calibrated fair price or current expected-return calculation.

Its combined estimate is about 0.64 points above today's 45.5 threshold. That small numerical disagreement cannot tell us the probability of finishing over, especially without an established scoring distribution or injury adjustment. We also have no measured sensitivity showing how a Williams return, a Bagent start or reduced quarterback mobility changes the estimate. CBS additionally reports that Chicago will be without running back Kyle Monangai; that is a role uncertainty to investigate, not permission to subtract an invented number of points.

The countercase and the next decision

The serious countercase is that uncertainty need not favor the under. A returning quarterback could expand Chicago's available offense, while a backup-led approach could alter pace or turnover exposure in either direction. Conversely, availability would not prove full mobility or normal workload. These are conditional mechanisms, not verified game plans.

The useful conclusion is a watchlist, not a directional recommendation. Confirmed quarterback availability, credible information about restrictions and a refreshed matchup estimate would make the assessment more specific. Until then, the unchanged threshold offers no demonstrated informational advantage, and the dated estimate cannot resolve the injury question. Price comparison is worthwhile; confusing a better price with a proven forecasting advantage is not.
