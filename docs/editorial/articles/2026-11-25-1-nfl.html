The hook: a high total for the holiday opener

Detroit hosts Chicago at Ford Field on Thursday, Nov. 26, at 1 p.m. ET on CBS, according to CBS Sports' Thanksgiving schedule piece published Tuesday. The game opens a slate that also includes Eagles at Cowboys and Chiefs at Bills. The market has put a high number on the opener: as of 7:19 AM EST Wednesday, quoted totals across 11 books sat between 55.0 and 55.5, with a median of 55.5.

The question here is narrow: how far does that price sit from Fourth & Value's experimental scoring estimate, and how much weight should the gap carry? The answer is less than the raw difference suggests.

What the books were showing

At 7:19 AM EST Wednesday, DraftKings, FanDuel, William Hill US, BetRivers, BetUS, BetOnline.ag, LowVig and Fanatics listed 55.5, while BetMGM, MyBookie.ag and Circa listed 55.0. BetMGM quoted 55.0 at -110 over and -108 under, and MyBookie.ag showed 55.0 at -110 on both sides. BetUS and BetOnline.ag each listed 55.5 at -110 on both sides.

Compared with our snapshot from 5:51 PM EST Tuesday, the median did not move. The one visible change was BetMGM, which had been at 55.5 and now showed 55.0. We do not have an opening price, so we cannot say where the number began, and a one-book half-point shift is thin evidence of a trend.

The half-point split between 55 and 55.5 is the only real shopping dispersion. On a total this high it matters mostly if the game lands exactly on 55, which is uncommon but not negligible.

The experimental estimate and how to read it

Fourth & Value's experimental NFL totals model, using game history through Nov. 23, produced a combined estimate of about 49.9 points, split roughly 27.0 for Detroit and 22.9 for Chicago. This is a saved, dated background estimate with no current price or expected-value calculation attached. It is a raw scoring estimate, not a calibrated fair price or win probability. It has not been validated against betting prices, and it does not include an injury adjustment.

The estimate sits roughly 5.6 points below the 55.5 consensus. That is a disagreement, and a disagreement is not a proven edge. A model without a demonstrated record against closing prices may be missing information the market has absorbed, such as lineup news, or may be underweighting recent scoring. We have no sensitivity testing that ties the gap to any particular input, so we do not assign it to one.

The team split shows the model leaning on Detroit's offense at home. Whether that reflects real matchup strength or model noise is unknown.

What the reporting does and does not add

The coverage adds little to the scoring question. CBS Sports' weekly picks column, published late Tuesday night, carries a headline saying Chicago loses on Thanksgiving, but the portion of the column available to us is mostly a joke about food and gives no matchup reasoning or comment on the total. The NFL.com report that Lions owner emeritus Martha Firestone Ford died at 101, as announced by the team Tuesday evening, is significant for the franchise but has no bearing on scoring.

As of 7:21 AM EST Wednesday, neither CBS Sports nor NFL.com had published injury or inactive information for either team, weather detail or lineup news in the pieces we reviewed. That is a limitation: injury news is among the most likely things to move a total on a short week.

The countercase and what would change the view

The case for the high number is straightforward. The market aggregates many books and likely reflects information our model lacks. A total above 55 implies both offenses are expected to score freely, and an estimate built on history through Nov. 23 may be stale against late news. We make no claim about holiday scoring patterns.

On the other side, a gap of this size is worth watching. If the estimate were validated and calibrated, it might suggest the market is pricing aggressively. It is not, so this is a watchlist item rather than a lean.

Our stance is a pass. Three developments would change that: inactive lists showing key skill-position or defensive absences, a market move that widens the gap between the 55 and 55.5 books, or a validated, injury-aware model run that still sits well below the market. Absent those, the supported conclusion is modest: the market is higher than our experimental number, and that says little about which is right.
