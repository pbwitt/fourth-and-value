Chicago’s decision is about preparation, not just clearance

Case Keenum is expected to start for Chicago against Philadelphia on Monday night, according to NFL Network’s September 28 report. Caleb Williams has been ruled out with a hamstring injury. Tyson Bagent cleared concussion protocol Sunday, but his limited preparation—only one practice, with the game-plan installation missed—helps explain why medical clearance has not translated into the starting assignment.

That creates a more specific question than whether a third-string quarterback weakens an offense: how much can Chicago execute with the quarterback who received the preparation? NFL Network reports that Keenum has not attempted a regular-season pass since December 2023. Familiarity with the plan may help with communication and decisions, but it does not establish his present ability to escape pressure or create explosive plays.

CBS also describes Keenum as the expected starter. We treat the overlapping quarterback accounts as one reported development, not separate confirmation. The actionable issue is whether Chicago’s limitations are already adequately reflected in a total centered on 41.5.

The countercase to an automatic under

Philadelphia’s offense also matters. CBS reports that Saquon Barkley is off the injury report after leaving much of the previous game with a stinger. His availability offers a potential route to sustained offense without requiring Chicago to contribute equally. The same preview reports Philadelphia averaging 5.3 yards per play, however, which does not establish an attack ready to overwhelm this total.

There is also a defensive uncertainty: Jonathan Greenard practiced fully Friday but remains questionable, according to CBS. A return could complicate Keenum’s protection and decision-making, although no quantified scoring effect is established. Disrupted Chicago possessions could suppress scoring—or create short fields for Philadelphia. A weak offense is not mechanically equivalent to a low combined score.

Our conclusion is a pass pending firmer lineup information and a current assessment that explicitly addresses Chicago’s quarterback situation. Confirmation of the starter, Greenard’s availability and changes in the actual line-price combination would warrant another look. The news supports caution about Chicago’s offense; it does not yet support paying any price for the under.
