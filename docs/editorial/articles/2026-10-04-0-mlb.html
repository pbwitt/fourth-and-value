A workload question after a narrow opener

San Diego enters October 4’s NLDS Game 2 trailing Milwaukee after a 3–2 loss, with Michael King scheduled to face Logan Henderson. That makes King’s workload a more useful analytical focus than a broad claim that the Padres must respond. The relevant question is whether San Diego lets him record at least 15 outs—not simply whether he pitches well.

Game 1 also left a concrete management consideration: Milwaukee used five relievers, while San Diego’s Adrian Morejon surrendered the decisive seventh-inning home run. Those events establish recent bullpen usage, not today’s availability or a guaranteed longer leash for King. Fourth & Value’s position is a watchlist, pending clearer lineup and workload information.

What the experimental forecast says

Our October 4 snapshot lists King over 14.5 pitcher outs at DraftKings at −133, quoted at 11:07 UTC. Fourth & Value’s experimental model projects 16.15 outs and a 72.3% probability of exceeding that threshold. It has not been validated against betting prices; these estimates are not a recommendation or a demonstrated betting advantage.

The distinction between the mean and the threshold matters. The projection is only 1.65 outs above the line, although the forecast distribution assigns a substantially higher probability than the quote’s roughly 57.1% raw implied probability. That disagreement deserves examination, not automatic acceptance. Three paired books support the snapshot’s market comparison, but wider price coverage does not establish forecast accuracy.

The model uses history through October 3. King’s recorded inputs include 15 recent starter outings, 17.29 outs per start across his last five, a 21.9% strikeout rate per batter faced and a 22.8% opponent strikeout rate per plate appearance. Those are context, not measured explanations of why this particular probability is elevated.

Efficiency and managerial discretion pull in different directions

The model’s pitcher framework examines the last 15 starts, including recent outs and pitches, batters faced, rate statistics and rest. Small samples shrink toward fixed league-like priors. Its matchup framework also includes postseason context. That structure is relevant to an outs forecast, but it does not demonstrate that the model captures today’s managerial decisions accurately.

Conditional on King avoiding long innings, a heavily used bullpen could strengthen the practical case for keeping him in. The countercase is equally serious: with San Diego already behind in the series, an early scoring threat could prompt a quicker intervention. Neither mechanism is established as today’s plan by the available reporting. Moreover, five innings completed and five innings started are different outcomes; an exit with two outs in the fifth would still lose an over-14.5 position.

The price matters more than the headline

At −133, the over requires approximately a 57.1% success rate to break even before considering forecast uncertainty. A more expensive quote would increase that requirement without changing the 15-out task. No alternative King price is available in our snapshot, so there is no basis here for promising a better entry elsewhere.

Nor should the unusual finish to Game 1 substitute for workload analysis. CBS Sports described Ty France’s ninth-inning fly ball striking a roof cable before being caught. That incident helps explain the opener’s drama, but supplies no evidence about King’s pitch count, command or removal threshold. The series score is observed; a supposed rebound effect is not.

What would change the assessment

Published batting orders were still pending in our snapshot. A confirmed order, credible reporting on King’s workload plan and clearer bullpen availability would make the projection easier to assess. A materially different price would also require a fresh comparison rather than carrying forward the current probability-price disagreement.

For now, King’s recent recorded workload supports studying the over, while postseason discretion provides a substantial reason for restraint. The experimental estimate is informative enough to monitor, but not validated enough against betting prices to turn this Game 2 watchlist into a recommendation.
