An offensive revival with a split scorecard

Atlanta beat Green Bay 35–14 at Lambeau Field on Thursday, delivering the offensive response it needed after an 0–2 start. For Fourth & Value, the review is mixed: Michael Penix Jr. stayed under the passing-attempts line highlighted in our preview, while Tucker Kraft went over the receptions line we had flagged for an under.

Those were conditional research leads drawn from Wednesday’s saved prices, not confirmed executable bets. We can grade their outcomes, but we should not rewrite the preview as a winning betting card. It also made no firm pick on the game total. The 49 combined points cleared Thursday morning’s market median of 43 by six.

Sources: Fourth & Value’s September 24 preview and saved predictions · Packers.com: Falcons 35, Packers 14

Penix did more with fewer throws

Our saved model projected 26.63 Penix attempts and assigned under 31.5 a 63.6% probability. The archived BetMGM price was -115. Penix finished with 25 attempts, 1.63 below our projection and comfortably below the line.

The important distinction is that low passing volume did not mean an ineffective offense. Penix completed 18 passes for 256 yards. Bijan Robinson supplied 194 rushing yards and two touchdowns, while Drake London caught nine passes for 194 yards. Atlanta could create substantial offense without asking its returning quarterback to throw repeatedly.

Our interpretation is that the run game and Atlanta’s growing lead supported the lower-volume outcome. That is consistent with the under, but it does not establish that the model anticipated this particular path. The preview discussed a cautious return from injury; the game delivered an offense capable of moving the ball efficiently. A correct result and a correct explanation are separate things.

Kraft’s under lost, even without a big yardage night

The other featured signal was Kraft under 3.5 receptions at an archived +130. Our model projected 2.28 catches and a 57.9% under probability. He finished with four receptions on eight targets for 26 yards. That is a loss for the stated under, and his catch total exceeded the projection by 1.72.

Green Bay’s predicament provided a very different volume environment. Jordan Love attempted 53 passes, while the Packers produced only 17 rushing yards. A tight end did not need explosive production to reach four receptions in that setting. Short completions still count.

The preview specifically warned that Jayden Reed’s absence could change target distribution. We cannot isolate how much that absence caused Kraft’s usage, but the concern belonged in the analysis. The review question is whether the model gave enough weight to receiving-role changes and a trailing game script—not whether 26 yards sounds like an under-worthy performance.

One win and one loss still cost money

At the saved prices, a hypothetical $100 stake on each signal would have returned $86.96 profit on Penix and lost $100 on Kraft: a net loss of $13.04 on $200 staked, or 6.52%. This is illustrative settlement math, not a claim that those prices remained available or that anyone placed the bets.

The game total deserves its own accounting. Our morning snapshot spanned 42.5 to 43.5; all those overs would have won at 49. But the preview treated 43 as a reference point and declined to force a direction. We do not get to claim an over winner after the fact, and the Penix under was never equivalent to a game-total under.

What this changes for the next review

Penix’s attempt projection was close; Kraft’s reception projection was too low in this game. Neither observation validates or invalidates a probability model on its own. A 63.6% forecast is assessed over many comparable predictions, not awarded full credit because one outcome went its way.

The useful lesson is practical: preserve the original number and price, record whether a signal became an actionable recommendation, and review the football mechanism alongside the result. Thursday gave us a successful volume signal, a losing reception signal and an offense that scored efficiently. Keeping all three in the record is how the next decision gets better.
