Indianapolis’ receiving options narrow

Indianapolis enters Sunday’s London matchup against Washington with a more concentrated receiving group: Alec Pierce is on injured reserve with an ankle injury, and Keenan Allen has been ruled out with a groin injury. CBS Sports’ October 4 preview reports that Josh Downs has caught 12 passes for 149 yards over the past two weeks. That combination makes the Colts’ ability to sustain their passing offense without two receivers the central scoring question—not simply whether missing players point toward a lower total.

The game is scheduled for 9:30 a.m. ET at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. Both teams enter at 1-2 after earning their first victories last week, as the NFL’s October 2 preview documents. Those wins establish the immediate context, but neither result alone tells us whether the offenses can generate enough scoring opportunities to clear the current total.

The board has converged, not uniformly moved

Our October 4 morning snapshot shows a total of 46.5 at all 11 observed sportsbooks. Compared with the October 3 snapshot at 22:50 UTC, four books—BetRivers, BetUS, Bovada and MyBookie—have moved from 47 to 46.5; the other seven remain at 46.5. The median therefore has not changed. This is a narrowing of book dispersion, rather than evidence of a market-wide half-point decline.

Execution still differs despite the identical threshold. LowVig lists both sides at -107, while BetMGM lists the over at -108 and under at -110. Caesars has the over at -112 and under at -108; BetRivers has -112 and -109, respectively. Those differences affect the cost of taking a position, but they do not establish which side is more likely to win. Nor can the receiver news be credited with causing the convergence: our evidence does not connect timestamped injury announcements to before-and-after quotes.

A dated scoring estimate supplies context, not a price

Fourth & Value’s saved estimate, using information through September 28, projects approximately 45.46 combined points: 23.73 for Washington and 21.72 for Indianapolis. This experimental estimate has not been validated against betting prices. It is dated background, not a fresh forecast incorporating Sunday’s availability information, and it supplies neither a calibrated win probability nor expected betting return.

Its combined scoring figure sits about 1.04 points below today’s 46.5 threshold. That arithmetic disagreement is not a proven advantage. In particular, injury adjustments are not established, so we cannot subtract points for Pierce or Allen, assign an offset for Downs, or claim those personnel developments changed the forecast. Washington’s projected contribution also cannot be treated as a measured assessment of Marcus Mariota’s current role; CBS reports that he will start again while Jayden Daniels continues recovering from his elbow injury.

The countercase keeps this on the watchlist

Receiver absences can constrain passing options, but Downs’ recent production is a serious countercase to assuming Indianapolis’ offense must deteriorate. A concentrated passing game could remain effective if its available receivers consistently provide workable targets. Conversely, recent catches and yards do not establish that Downs can replace every function of the missing players. The available evidence supports monitoring that mechanism, not forecasting his targets or inventing a player-prop price.

The conclusion is a pass for now. A higher total would widen the numerical gap to the dated estimate; a lower one would narrow it, without resolving the validation problem. Confirmed game-day availability could sharpen the personnel picture, while a fresh forecast with measured injury sensitivity and betting-price validation would materially improve the analytical basis. Until then, the uniform 46.5 board is more actionable as a watchlist reference than as a recommendation.
