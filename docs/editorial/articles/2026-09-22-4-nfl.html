Two absences, more than one vacancy

The Chargers are sending David Njoku and Charlie Kolar to injured reserve. Jim Harbaugh's September 21 update identified a fibula injury for Njoku and a forearm injury for Kolar. At that announcement, Oronde Gadsden was the only remaining tight end on the active roster, with personnel additions under consideration.

That creates a substantive matchup question before Los Angeles visits Buffalo on Sunday, September 27, at 1 p.m. ET. Fourth & Value's question is not simply who inherits the missing targets. It is which blocking and receiving assignments the remaining personnel can handle together. More opportunity for Gadsden could coexist with fewer effective options for the offense. That distinction matters when evaluating a game total rather than an individual player's workload.

A receiving replacement is not a complete replacement

The Chargers' July position preview outlined the complementary skills in this room: Gadsden brought a 2025 receiving record of 49 catches, 664 yards and three touchdowns, while Kolar supplied an established blocking component. Those historical numbers describe Gadsden's demonstrated receiving ability, not a projection for his new workload.

Our conditional reading is that Mike McDaniel now faces an assignment problem. Using more three-receiver formations could free Gadsden to attack coverage, but it would change the run-pass combinations Buffalo must defend. Preserving heavier formations with another blocker could maintain those combinations without giving Gadsden every vacated route. Asking him to help protect Justin Herbert would create another gap between playing time and receiving opportunity.

None of those deployments is established yet. The important evidence will be who handles the blocking assignments and whether Gadsden is released into routes—not simply whether he moves up the depth chart. That follows from the complementary roles the team described before these injuries.

The interior matchup is still conditional

The personnel disruption extends to left guard. Harbaugh said Kayode Awosika would miss time with a fibula injury and identified Trevor Penning as the replacement if Trey Pipkins III remains unavailable with his knee injury. That qualification matters: the starting combination is not settled.

Buffalo's corresponding uncertainty is Ed Oliver. September 20 reporting described his hip injury as day-to-day, with a possible return against Los Angeles. The Bills also highlighted Deone Walker's interior contribution after Oliver missed their previous game. An Oliver absence therefore would not establish a favorable matchup by itself.

The mechanism to watch is whether Buffalo can disrupt the interior without committing extra rushers. If it can, Los Angeles may need additional protection while facing more defenders in coverage. If the replacement line holds up, Gadsden's receiving opportunities become easier to realize. Those are conditional football outcomes, not established injury adjustments.

Why the injuries do not automatically favor the under

The strongest countercase starts with Buffalo's contribution. The Bills just won 41–31 against Detroit despite Oliver's absence. Meanwhile, Los Angeles expects defensive tackle Dalvin Tomlinson to miss time with a hamstring injury. Offensive disruption on one side does not impose a ceiling on the other team's scoring.

There is also room for the Chargers to improve without becoming a complete offense. They committed three turnovers against Las Vegas, while Gadsden caught a 10-yard touchdown in that game. Those observations establish both execution problems and an available receiving option; neither supplies a dependable scoring baseline.

Cleaner possessions and receiving-oriented personnel could help Los Angeles sustain drives. Conversely, another disjointed performance could give Buffalo favorable field position. Both are credible ways for this matchup to resist a simple injury-driven under thesis. The two-game season is too young to treat the Chargers' results as their fixed offensive identity.

What would change the conclusion

This analysis does not establish a model fair total, calibrated scoring distribution, player projection or quantified injury adjustment. Game-total quotes also cannot establish a Gadsden receptions or yardage forecast.

The next decision points are the replacement tight-end plan, the left-guard combination and Oliver's practice progression. Evidence that another player can absorb blocking duties while Gadsden consistently runs routes would strengthen the offensive countercase. Evidence that Los Angeles must repeatedly retain eligible receivers in protection would strengthen the concern about drive sustainability. The unresolved personnel questions are documented; their scoring consequences remain conditional.

Fourth & Value's conclusion is a watchlist and a pass at this stage. The role change is real. An advantage over the recorded total has not been demonstrated.
