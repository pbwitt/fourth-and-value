Two injury questions, not one automatic under

Aaron Judge entered the injured list September 19 with a right calf strain. MLB and independent Associated Press reporting establish his absence. But the Yankees’ offensive outlook for September 22, 2026 is not exclusively about subtraction: their September 20 update identified Jazz Chisholm Jr.’s thumb injury as a possible Tuesday activation.

For Fourth & Value, that combination makes the Rays–Yankees evening total a lineup watchlist, not an injury-driven under recommendation. A confirmed absence and a possible return are different inputs. Neither should be converted into an invented run adjustment, and an activation would not by itself establish a starting assignment or an unrestricted workload.

The relevant question is who receives the available plate appearances. If the returning player starts, the comparison is with the hitter displaced from that particular batting order—not with an imaginary full-strength lineup. Until that substitution is known, the injury story supplies a reason to investigate the total, not a demonstrated pricing error.

Strong starters, but no usable totals forecast

MLB lists Tampa Bay’s Drew Rasmussen (2.72 ERA) against New York’s Max Fried (2.63). Fried worked seven innings in his previous start. Those observations support examining a low-scoring scenario, but they do not establish tonight’s expected innings or the probability of finishing below the posted total.

The matchup mechanism is workload allocation. If both starters pitch deep, fewer outs must come from whichever relievers remain available after the afternoon game. If either exits early, the nightcap becomes more dependent on that intervening workload. Fourth & Value would not label either bullpen depleted before observing those appearances, nor assume that one long previous outing guarantees another.

Our model evidence imposes a separate limit: we do not have a validated game-specific totals forecast to support this bet. Moreover, the regular-season totals audit failed its stated validation criteria. Across 393 games and 1,965 threshold evaluations, it reports a Brier score of 0.232836 against a 0.230632 reference, with Brier skill of -0.0096. Its expected calibration error, 0.060374, also exceeds the recorded 0.06 ceiling. These are reported audit results, not independently reproduced findings.

Accordingly, totals should be treated as research-only for this matchup. The threshold evaluations are not 1,965 independent games, and successful player-prop categories do not validate a full-game total. The audit also conditions on actual starters and original batting orders, lacks archived pregame announcements, and includes no explicit injury or weather adjustment. It cannot justify subtracting an arbitrary amount for an absent hitter or manufacturing a fair price for this matchup.

The countercase—and what would change the pass

MLB’s nightcap lineup page still showed both batting orders as undetermined when checked. That missing information matters because the strongest countercase to an under is not necessarily a spectacular offensive performance. At the recorded threshold, a seven-run game defeats every under ticket discussed here. One difficult inning could overwhelm otherwise effective starting pitching.

There are opposing doubleheader scenarios. Heavy afternoon relief usage could increase exposure to less-preferred pitching options at night. Conversely, resting regular hitters could weaken an evening batting order. These are conditional mechanisms, not confirmed managerial plans, and they can offset one another. Simply attaching an over or under label to the doubleheader would skip the information that determines its relevance.

The next review should follow the official activation decision, both evening batting orders, and the afternoon pitching ledger. A returning hitter in a substantial role would require reassessing the offensive-loss argument. Additional rest absences could strengthen that argument, while heavy relief usage could weaken it. Any such assessment must use refreshed prices.

The present conclusion is a pass. Better lineup information could support a more specific watchlist, but it would not repair the failed totals validation or create a missing forecast. A bet requires an adequately supported scoring estimate and a suitable price—not merely a persuasive injury narrative.
