The hook: a thin Rams offense and a returning Packers pass rusher

The first Thanksgiving Eve game in NFL history sends Green Bay to Los Angeles at 8:00 PM EST Wednesday, and the reporting centers on who is missing. CBS Sports reported at 7:00 AM EST Wednesday that Puka Nacua is likely out for the year after surgery on a lower-body injury, that Terrance Ferguson is likely out as well, and that Davante Adams remains out with a hamstring problem. NFL.com, in a Tuesday item, described Nacua's season-ending knee surgery and said he will finish with only seven games played in 2026. The two pieces describe the injury a little differently, so we rely only on the shared point that he is done for the season.

On the other side, CBS reported that Micah Parsons makes his season debut for Green Bay after tearing an ACL late last year. Both teams are coming off byes. As of 7:20 AM EST Wednesday, neither team had released an official inactive list in the reporting we checked, so the final availability picture was incomplete.

What the market shows

Totals were tightly clustered across eleven books. The median was 44.0, with a range of 43.5 to 44.5. At 7:19 AM EST Wednesday, DraftKings listed 44.5 with the over at -107 and the under at -112. FanDuel also listed 44.5, at +100 over and -120 under. LowVig.ag listed 44.0 at -107 on both sides, and BetRivers listed 43.5 with the over at -114 and the under at -107.

Every book except DraftKings held its number from 5:51 PM EST Tuesday, and DraftKings moved from 43.5 to 44.5. That is one book moving, not a market-wide shift, and without timestamped quotes bracketing a specific news item we do not attribute the move to any injury report.

The experimental estimate

Fourth & Value's experimental totals model, with inputs through Nov. 23, estimated about 47.9 combined points: roughly 26.4 for the Rams and 21.5 for the Packers. This is a saved scoring estimate. It has not been validated against betting prices, it is not a calibrated fair price or win probability, and it carries no established injury adjustment. The reporting does not establish when each injury became known, so we cannot say how much of the receiver news the estimate reflects, and we do not claim any input drove the number.

The gap of roughly three to four points above the posted lines is a disagreement, not a proven edge. A model without an established injury adjustment can run high against a market that has priced in the receiver losses.

Matchup mechanisms

CBS highlighted a lopsided Green Bay profile: the Packers are last in the league at 3.1 yards per carry, and their 670 rushing yards trail the next-closest team by 124. That points toward a pass-heavy script built around Jordan Love targeting Christian Watson and Matthew Golden, which can produce quick scores but also incompletions and clock stoppages. The same piece said the Rams allow 3.9 yards per carry, so Green Bay's ground game has little room to change the picture.

For Los Angeles, CBS said the team will need its defense and running backs. A run-leaning offense with weakened receivers tends to shorten possessions, which is a conditional argument for the lower side. Pete Prisco, also at CBS, picked the Packers and said the outcome hinges on Green Bay's line against the Rams' pass rush, while expecting the defense to keep it close. Both pieces come from one outlet, so they are not independent confirmation of each other.

Countercase and price sensitivity

The case for a higher total is that Green Bay's offense is described as one of the league's most explosive, and a model reading near 48 would be right if the Rams' offense proves more resilient than the receiver losses suggest. Parsons could strengthen a defense that is already good, which cuts the other way.

Half-point differences matter at 44: the gap between 43.5 and 44.5 is meaningful for push and juice, and the prices show little room, with DraftKings' under at -112 and BetRivers' over at -114. A lone book at 43.5 says little by itself.

Conclusion and what would change it

Our read is a watchlist, not a pick. The reporting supports a Rams offense missing key receivers and a Packers team getting a defensive star back, while the experimental estimate sits above the market without validation. We would revisit if official inactive lists showed more Rams skill players available than reported, if Parsons' snap count looked limited, or if timestamped quotes showed the market moving toward or away from 47. Until then, the useful finding is that the market is consistent across books and the model disagreement is unproven.
