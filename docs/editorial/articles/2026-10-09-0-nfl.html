Eagles–Jaguars: Jurgens trade tests Philadelphia’s scoring floor

Philadelphia’s offensive-line reshuffle raises a scoring question, but the total has stayed steady. A dated scoring estimate offers context—not a validated reason to oppose the market.

By Fourth & Value · Oct 09, 2026 at 07:10 AM ET

A dated market assessment. Prices and availability can change after publication.

A personnel change, not a demonstrated market reaction

Philadelphia heads into Sunday’s London matchup against Jacksonville after trading Cam Jurgens to Baltimore. NFL.com reported on October 7 that the teams announced the transaction, removing a lineman who had previously started at center and had moved to guard this season. That makes Philadelphia’s ability to sustain possessions a concrete question for this matchup, rather than a generic concern about international travel.

Our October 9 snapshot puts the total between 41.5 and 42 across 11 books, with a median of 42. Every matched book retained its earlier number from the October 9 comparison snapshot. That establishes recent stability, not the market’s response to the trade: both observations came after the announcement. There is no timestamped pre-trade comparison here that would justify saying the transaction pushed the total down. CBS Sports’ October 9 preview identifies the Eagles–Jaguars game as Sunday’s London fixture.

The relevant question is continuity, not reputation

The league’s reporting describes an offensive line already operating with disruption. An injury to Landon Dickerson prompted Jurgens’ move to guard, where his performance through four weeks ranked near the bottom of the position by the grading cited in that report. It also notes Lane Johnson’s Week 4 absence. Those are historical circumstances, not confirmation of either player’s availability against Jacksonville; the available excerpts do not establish their current game status or Philadelphia’s replacement alignment.

The conditional mechanism matters. If the new configuration struggles with protection exchanges or interior pressure, Philadelphia could lose drives through sacks, hurried throws and difficult third downs. If the replacement arrangement functions better than the struggling combination described in the report, however, the trade need not reduce scoring. Removing a familiar starter and weakening an offense are not interchangeable claims. Without a confirmed lineup, the direction and size of any effect remain unresolved.

What the dated scoring estimate actually says

Fourth & Value’s saved estimate, using information through October 5, projected approximately 47.1 combined points: 27.3 for Jacksonville and 19.9 for Philadelphia. This is an experimental scoring estimate that has not been validated against betting prices. It is dated background, not a fresh prediction incorporating the October 7 trade, and it provides neither a calibrated fair total nor a current expected return.

Its useful feature is the distribution of scoring between the teams. Jacksonville accounts for the larger projected contribution, so Philadelphia’s line is not the only question relevant to the total. Even a restrained Eagles scoring performance would not, by itself, settle the combined-points outcome. Conversely, the estimate’s higher aggregate cannot establish that the observed market is wrong: injury adjustments are not established, and there is no measured sensitivity showing how a personnel change would alter this forecast. The gap is a disagreement requiring investigation, not a proven betting advantage.

Price sensitivity and the case for waiting

The half-point dispersion is consequential even without a directional forecast. In our snapshot, BetMGM offered 41.5 with over -108 and under -110; BetUS offered 42 with both sides -110. At exactly 42 combined points, the former over wins while the latter pushes; the former under loses while the latter pushes. That settlement distinction is real, but it does not supply the missing probability of that outcome.

The serious countercase to a lower-scoring interpretation is that Philadelphia’s reshuffle could improve a problematic unit, while Jacksonville supplies enough offense to offset limited Eagles production. The countercase to leaning on the higher background estimate is equally substantial: it predates the trade and lacks established injury adjustments. Confirmed offensive-line roles, current availability and a refreshed matchup estimate would materially improve the assessment. Until then, this is a watchlist game: a stable market facing unresolved personnel questions, not a forced selection.
