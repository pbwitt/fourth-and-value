Cardinals at Chiefs: A 40.5 Total Meets Arizona's Fourth Starting Quarterback in Four Weeks

Josh Johnson starts for Arizona at Kansas City. The total sits near 40.5 while an experimental, unvalidated scoring estimate from Nov. 16 data is higher.

By Fourth & Value · Nov 22, 2026 at 07:17 AM ET

A dated market assessment. Prices and availability can change after publication.

The news hook: another quarterback in Arizona

Arizona will start Josh Johnson at Kansas City on Sunday, its fourth different starting quarterback in four weeks. CBS Sports reported on Saturday, Nov. 21, at 3:12 PM EST that rookie Carson Beck was ruled out with back spasms. NFL.com's report covered the same decision and described Johnson as a 40-year-old journeyman making his 12th career start. Both outlets describe one team decision, so we treat them as one underlying report on Beck's status, not as independent confirmation of each other.

The sequence is unusual. Per the reporting, Jacoby Brissett started Week 8 and was benched, Gardner Minshew started Week 9 and was hurt, and Beck started Week 10. Johnson played for coach Mike LaFleur with the Jets in 2021 and joined Arizona last week, which gives him some familiarity but little time with this offense.

What the market shows

Our snapshot shows a tight total. At 7:17 AM EST Sunday, DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, Circa and several others listed 40.5. LowVig and BetOnline listed 40. Caesars quoted 40.5 at -115 over and -105 under, while Circa and BetOnline were at -110 on both sides and LowVig at -107 each way.

The prior check, at 6:18 PM EST Saturday, had LowVig and BetOnline at 41 and the others at 40.5. So the two books that differed moved down half a point, while the median stayed at 40.5. The quarterback ruling was reported earlier on Saturday, and we have no timestamped before-and-after quotes tied to it, so we do not say the news caused any move. We also do not have an opening line.

The experimental estimate and its limits

Fourth & Value's experimental NFL totals model, using data through Nov. 16, estimated about 44.0 combined points: roughly 25.2 for Kansas City and 18.8 for Arizona. This is a dated background estimate, not a current price comparison. It is a raw scoring estimate, not a calibrated fair price or win probability, and it has not been validated against betting prices. The model also states that injury adjustments are not established, so we cannot say it reflects Johnson replacing Beck.

The gap of about 3.5 points above the 40.5 median is therefore a disagreement, not a proven edge. It does not tell us the over is mispriced, and we are not offering it as a recommendation. The most it says is that, before the quarterback change was handled, the model's team-level scoring inputs were more optimistic than the market.

Matchup mechanics

Kansas City's side of the number has a documented mechanism. CBS Sports reported on Friday, Nov. 20, at 11:15 AM EST that the Chiefs' run game with Kenneth Walker III has changed their offense. The piece notes that Kansas City generated 20-plus yard gains on only 4.8% of plays in 2024 and 2025, tied for 29th in the NFL, after defenses forced them into shorter, lower-yield drives. A better ground game could help Kansas City sustain drives and also shorten the game by using clock, which pulls in different directions for a total.

For Arizona, the issue is stability, not a proven talent drop. A quarterback who has had a week with the team, a coach saying the offense is returning to its basics, and an undrafted backup with no regular-season snaps all point to a limited, conservative script. That is our inference from the reporting, not a measured result.

Countercase and price sensitivity

The case for a higher-scoring game is that the model has Kansas City near 25 points, and a Chiefs offense with a working run game could reach that even if Arizona scores little. The case for a lower one is that a 40-year-old on short notice, with a basics-first plan, could stall drives and shorten the game. The market's 40.5 may already include both, and the half-point differences between books are small. At Caesars, the -115 over price makes that side costlier than at Circa or BetOnline, so the effective price differs by book even when the number matches.

Because the model's input predates the confirmed quarterback change and carries no injury adjustment, we would not lean on the gap. Our stance is a pass or watchlist, not a forced pick.

What would change the view

A few things would move us. A validated model update that accounts for Johnson would matter most, along with evidence that Arizona's offense will go beyond conservative play-calling. Timestamped quotes showing a consistent move after the Beck announcement would also help us understand how the market priced it. Absent that, the supported conclusion is narrow: the total is steady at 40 to 40.5 across the books checked, the experimental estimate is higher, and the quarterback uncertainty is the main reason to avoid reading that gap as value. As of 7:17 AM EST Sunday, kickoff was set for 1:00 PM EST, and we had no further lineup news beyond the reports cited here.
