Yankees–Rays: Peralta tests an experimental forecast above 6.5 runs

Tampa Bay turns to Freddy Peralta after a one-run series opener. Our experimental forecast points higher than the 6.5 total, but recent pitching form and unsettled lineups warrant caution.

By Fourth & Value · Oct 05, 2026 at 07:10 AM ET

A dated market assessment. Prices and availability can change after publication.

A low-scoring opener, a different pitching test

The Yankees enter Monday’s ALDS Game 2 against Tampa Bay after losing Saturday’s opener 1–0 and managing only one hit. The Rays have announced Freddy Peralta as their next starter, according to MLB.com’s October 4 preview. That creates a concrete question for the total: does New York’s offensive shutdown describe the next matchup, or primarily the exceptional outing it just faced?

Our October 5 snapshot has the total at 6.5 across all 11 observed sportsbooks. Fourth & Value’s experimental forecast projects 8.30 combined runs. It has not been validated against betting prices, so that disagreement is a question to investigate—not a recommendation. Peralta’s recent performance gives the lower market total a substantial baseball argument behind it.

What the forecast actually measures

The experimental estimate assigns a 64.1% probability to more than 6.5 runs. Its inputs include recent scoring averages of 5.00 runs per game for New York and 4.46 for Tampa Bay, alongside recent starter runs-allowed rates of 2.62 per nine innings for the away side and 4.54 for the home side. Those are model inputs, not predictions of either starter’s Game 2 performance.

The framework uses the last 40 team games and last 15 pitcher starts, with history through October 4. Its weights were trained through August 5. That makes the forecast more informative than simply carrying forward Saturday’s score, but it also introduces a possible tension: a multi-start pitching window can describe a different level of performance than the most recent month. MLB.com reports that Peralta went 5–0 with a 0.79 ERA in September after joining Tampa Bay from the Mets in August.

We do not have a measured sensitivity test isolating those pitching inputs. It would therefore be unjustified to say Peralta’s longer-window rate caused the higher projection.

The number is stable; the price is not identical

The total remained 6.5 at the 10 books matched against our previous snapshot from October 4. That is a stored comparison, not an opening line, and it does not establish a market response to the starter announcement.

At the same threshold, our selected current quotes show the over at -112 at MyBookie.ag and -118 at FanDuel. Those prices require approximately 52.8% and 54.1% success, respectively, before allowing for forecast uncertainty. The difference matters even when every book posts the same run total: a bettor pays more for the identical scoring outcome at the latter quote.

The experimental probability sits above both thresholds, but arithmetic alone does not establish a profitable opportunity. Its apparent separation from the market must survive errors in pitching assessment, postseason usage and batting-order assumptions. The Peralta announcement sharpens those questions rather than resolving them.

Stanton’s return is not a full-lineup upgrade

Giancarlo Stanton’s appearance adds another reason to distinguish roster availability from expected offensive contribution. CBS Sports reports that he returned to game action in the opener after missing regular-season action since April 24 because of three separate calf injuries. He is on the postseason roster as a pinch-hitter and entered Game 1 in the ninth inning.

That evidence supports a limited offensive role, not an assumption that Stanton will supply a full game of plate appearances. Our forecast is still awaiting published batting orders. A late pinch-hit opportunity can matter, but its existence should not be treated as equivalent to a healthy middle-order starter. Nor does one hit against Drew Rasmussen establish that the Yankees’ recent scoring average is irrelevant.

What would resolve the disagreement

The strongest countercase is straightforward: Tampa Bay follows Rasmussen’s dominant opener with a pitcher coming off an outstanding September, while New York’s returning power bat has a constrained documented role. Those facts can support a low-scoring expectation without requiring Saturday’s result to repeat.

For now, this is a watchlist matchup. Published batting orders, clearer expected pitching workloads and a refreshed quote would improve the assessment. A forecast that remains elevated after those details are incorporated would merit further examination, but without betting-price validation it would still not constitute a recommendation.
