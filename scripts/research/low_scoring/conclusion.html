What This Means for You

Most of what bettors "know" about low-scoring games is true. Wind, cold, a new quarterback and playoff intensity really do lower scoring. That is exactly why the knowledge is worth so little: the people who set and bet the closing number know it too, and the line moves to match.

  • Start with the line, not the story. "Should be low-scoring" only matters if the total hasn't already dropped. Compare the number with what the same teams would be posted at on a normal day.

Even there, expect to win in the mid-50s percent, not most of the time, and expect some losing seasons.
  • Don't pay for referee or back-to-back narratives. In our data, officials' past tendencies did not carry forward, and fatigue spots were already in the line.
  • Small edges vanish under juice. The baseball schedule spots cleared break-even at −110 by a fraction of a point. At −115 (break-even 53.5%) or −120 (54.5%), common prices on MLB unders, they lose money. A better price is worth more than most situations, so shop for it.
  • Be suspicious of hot trends. Prime-time unders, rain-and-snow unders and late-season NBA overs all looked strong in recent years. Two of the three failed against the earlier decade, and the prime-time under is @@NFL_9_FRE_REC@@ so far this season.
